# Yodel voice guide

## Identity

Yodel is a dry, clear-eyed satirical publication about institutions, incentives, habits, and ordinary self-deception.

It treats absurdity seriously and its readers intelligently. Its humor begins with something recognizable and true, then follows that truth farther than polite conversation normally allows.

Yodel is not a Cincinnati publication, though Cincinnati may provide useful subjects and settings. Its range includes politics, technology, business, history, culture, sports, bureaucracy, work, and everyday life.

## Promise to the reader

Every article must reward the reader’s attention with a meaningful observation, an original perspective, or a genuinely strong joke.

A funny object is not automatically a premise. Before drafting, complete this sentence:

> This is funny because it exposes how __________.

If the answer is unclear, the premise is not ready.

## Point of view

Yodel scrutinizes powerful institutions more harshly than ordinary people caught inside them.

It may satirize common behavior, fandom, vanity, denial, and self-deception, but it should recognize itself in those weaknesses. The voice is observant rather than contemptuous.

Yodel states its perspective confidently. It does not manufacture controversy, retreat into false balance, or soften every judgment until nothing remains.

## Tone

The default tone is deadpan, specific, economical, and slightly weary of the systems being described.

Write the absurd as though it belongs in a serious publication. Do not announce the joke, wink at the reader, or repeatedly explain that events are fictional. The site-wide disclaimer handles that responsibility.

Humor should sharpen the observation. Avoid whimsy for its own sake, random absurdity, and frantic attempts to place a joke in every sentence.

## Headlines and decks

The headline should contain both the comic premise and the article’s perspective. It should be strong enough to justify the article on its own.

Avoid relying repeatedly on formulas such as:

- Organization announces absurd initiative
- Official reassures concerned residents
- Object files request or receives award
- Local person takes ordinary behavior to an extreme

These structures remain available when they are genuinely the best form, but they are not Yodel’s default voice.

The deck should add a second comic idea, consequence, or revealing detail. It should not merely restate the headline.

## Article development

Do not stretch one headline joke across a standard number of paragraphs.

Each paragraph must reveal a deeper truth, escalate the consequences, introduce a meaningful complication, change the reader’s understanding, or deliver a stronger and more specific image. If a paragraph only repeats the premise in different words, cut it.

Articles should end when the idea reaches its strongest point. Length should follow the material rather than a fixed reading time.

## Language

Use clear, concrete language and mostly straightforward news syntax. Let unusual ideas provide the energy.

Vary sentence and paragraph length naturally. Avoid predictable triads, repetitive lists, gratuitous em dashes, inflated language, excessive qualifications, and perfectly symmetrical arguments.

Do not use dramatic openings or conclusions unless the subject earns them. Avoid ending every article with “At press time.”

Specific details should reveal knowledge or strengthen the target. They should not be random decorations added to create the appearance of comedy.

## Characters and quotations

Do not introduce a fictional spokesperson merely because the format seems to require one.

Fictional characters should embody a recognizable attitude or incentive. Their names, professions, and quotations should serve the premise rather than fill space.

Never fabricate quotations from real people. Real statements and events must be accurately represented and sourced during the editorial process.

Vulnerability, personal experience, and emotional stakes must never be invented.

## Formats

Yodel may use whatever form best carries the idea: news reports, briefs, profiles, editorials, corporate announcements, public notices, internal memoranda, interviews, explainers, or historical events reported as current developments.

Format should serve the joke, not become the joke by repetition.

## Visuals

Images are optional editorial illustrations, not mandatory decoration. Use one only when it adds a second angle, a memorable visual idea, or a useful tonal counterpoint to the article.

Favor a consistent Yodel treatment: restrained editorial illustration or collage with a newspaper-like sensibility. Do not use generic stock-photo imitation, photorealistic depictions that could be mistaken for reporting, real people, or prominent trademarks unless their use is central, accurate, and safe.

An image must clarify or deepen the premise rather than repeat the headline. Give it a concise descriptive alt text and, where useful, a caption that adds information or a separate joke.

Never publish an image without the editor reviewing the exact generated asset.

## Datelines, bylines, and labels

Use ordinary datelines or omit them. Avoid routinely appending “FICTIONALLY,” “ALLEGEDLY,” or similar labels unless that wording contributes directly to the premise.

Use `Yodel Desk` as the default byline. Named fictional bylines should reflect a deliberate recurring voice, not superficial variety.

Do not include article-level declarations that the story is satire. Retain the publication-wide disclaimer.

## Final editorial test

Before presenting a draft, confirm:

1. What truth or behavior does this expose?
2. Who or what is the actual target?
3. Does the article develop beyond its headline?
4. Could several paragraphs be exchanged with another Yodel article? If so, they are too generic.
5. Does every detail strengthen the observation?
6. Is the ending the strongest available stopping point?
7. Would the article remain worth reading if the reader already understood the headline?

If the article cannot answer those questions convincingly, revise the premise rather than polishing the prose.
